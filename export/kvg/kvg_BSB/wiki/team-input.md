# The team's input

**For the Kuni translation team.** Write here anything you want the drafts to get right: a word you use, a spelling, a
name, a rule, a correction to a draft verse, an answer to one of the [questions](questions.md). Plain sentences in English
are enough; quotations in Kuni as you write them. Nobody needs to touch the store files.

How it works:
- add an item at the end, as `### T<next number> · <date> · <your name or "the team">`, then a line `Status: new`, then your
  words (as long as you like; examples help: chapter and verse, the wrong form, the right one);
- a curation step turns each new item into entries of the project's store and writes on the status line what it did
  (`entered: …`, or `question back: …` when something is unclear);
- from then on every drafting agent sees your decision first, marked as binding, and no agent may change it. If you change
  your mind, add a new item: only the team changes its own decisions.

Your words stay as you wrote them; only the status lines are written by the curation step.

### T1 · 2026-10-26 · Kuni team (question page, answer code)
Status: entered: claim:ndø-.team, claim:prohibitive.team, hyphen.team (2026-10-26)
Question page (ndo): What does ndø- before a hyphen do?
ndø- is one of over 700 portmanteau verb subject prefixes. 
It is normally 3rd person (sg or pl). Depending on the tone pattern it is either past/present or future, statement or question. 
However, if the verb has a low tone (grave accent, U+0300) on its second-last syllable and a rising tone (macron-acute, U+1DC4) on its last syllable it is a 2nd person ‘do not’ command.
(The hyphen itself does not have any grammatical significance. We use it in long verbs when the prefix consists of many syllables or when the verb stem starts with a vowel to make reading easier.)

### T2 · 2026-10-26 · Kuni team (question page, answer code)
Status: entered: claim:be-.team, obligative.team, negative_obligative.team, sequential_obligative.team (2026-10-26)
Question page (be): What does be- mean on a verb?
be- is obligative. 
With low tone (grave accent, U+0300) on its first syllable and high tone (acute accent, U+0301) on its last, the verb is affirmative.
With grave accent (U+0300) on its second-last syllable and a macron-acute (U+1DC4) on its last syllable the verb is negative, ‘must not’. 
With a macron (U+0304) on its last syllable before the stem it is sequential (affirmative).

### T4 · 2026-10-26 · Kuni team (question page, answer code)
Status: entered: claim:we.team (2026-10-26)
Question page (we): Does Kuni have two words for "we"?
Kuni does not have two words for ‘we’. BUT, 1pl inclusive vs exclusive is distinguished in the subject verb prefix. Where this prefix is or contains ‹ni› it is exclusive. Where it is or contains ‹zi› it is inclusive.

### T12 · 2026-10-26 · Kuni team (question page, answer code)
Status: entered: formula:and_he_said_seq.team, formula:and_they_said_seq.team, formula:and_i_said_seq.team, formula:thought.team, formula:they_thought.team, ate_ablative.team (2026-10-26)
Question page (voe): Vø- or vøe-: what does the e add?
Both are sequential forms of the verb ‘say’. 
‹vø̄-ein› ‘and he/she said’
‹vø̄-einim ‘and they said’
‹vø̄e-ein› ‘and I said’
But different meaning after ‹até›:
‹até vø̄e-ein› ‘he/she thought/assumed’
‹até vø̄e-einim› ‘they thought/assumed’
(Like ‹av› the particle ‹ate› requires a referential/ablative form of the verb.)
