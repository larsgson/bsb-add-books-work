# Verbs

A verb is prefix(es) + stem + suffix. The prefixes mark subject, object and direction; the
tones mark tense/mood. Learn forms from model verses rather than building them.

### Subject prefixes (narrative past)
Each subject prefix is one portmanteau piece (T1): person and number fused with tense and statement/question,
which the tone tells apart; a 1pl prefix with zi is inclusive, one with ni exclusive (T4; m:zi-:verb.team, m:ni-:verb.team).
- ge- (he): "genøwáv" (Luk 2:4), "genanim" (Act 7:4), "ge-ewag" (Act 7:29), "geyagam"
  (Jhn 7:42).
- gu- (she): "gunøwav" (Luk 1:39 "vø̄wav" / 1:40 "gumø-ón"), "gu-ewag" (Luk 1:41), "guni-eín"
  (Luk 1:25), "gui-ova" (she gave birth, Luk 1:57), "guyagam" (who lived, Luk 4:26),
  "gunøqaván" (she went back, Jhn 11:28), "gunduwav" (she came). The gu- also appears when
  the object is a woman and the subject a man: "ndego enzol ezoqam gumbo-ein" (the angel said
  to her, Luk 1:30), "Yesu gumbo-qavøiu" (Jhn 4:13). With a plural subject the plural wins:
  "ndigu gimbo-qavøinam" (they answered her, Luk 1:61), "gimbo-einim" (Act 12:15).
- gi- (they): "ginøzáv" (Luk 2:22), "giyogem" (Luk 1:58), "gipakhaez" (they died),
  "gi꞉goam" (they were), "gini-einím" (they said).
- qa- (it, inanimate): "qafakhan" (Luk 2:2), "qagoám" (it was), "qanøngáz" (it began).
- 1sg: "qæmat" / "qamat" (I went), "nogó" (I did/have, Luk 15:18), "qæ-eivat" (I wept,
  Rev 5:4), "nø-ewág" (I heard, Rev 16:7); 2sg: "qoqo-" ("qoqoni-matanam", Luk 2:48);
  2pl: "qazo-" ("qazo-apat", Luk 7:24); 1pl: "qati-" ("qati-einím", Act 21:14).

### Direction
- -nø- thither (away): "genøwáv", "ginøzáv", "genøqaván" (Luk 1:23).
- -ndo-/-ndu- hither (towards the speaker/centre): "ndowáv" (came), "gendoqavan" (came back,
  Luk 15:30), "ginduqavaz" (came back, glossary), "gundu-qavøiu" (she replied, Luk 1:38),
  "gendo-itumb" (he brought us out, Act 7:40).

### Sequential vø̄-
"and then ...": no gender/number: "genøwáv, vømø̄qom" (went and settled, Act 7:4),
"ndø-okɨ́, vø̄nanim" (married and died, Mrk 12:20), "vømū-pakhaez" (and they died, Mrk 12:22),
"gè-eivám, Pol sasa ndømøvøinam neka vø̄ngenøtømem" (Act 20:37).

### Object marking
- mbo-/mbøe- (him/her), zø-/zømbe- (them, you pl), nø- (me), ni- (us), qa-/taqa- (you sg):
  "gembo-ein" (said to him, Luk 1:30), "gezø-ein" (to them, Luk 2:10), "āv gezømbe-eín nqǽgo"
  (Luk 2:15), "guzømbe-ein" (she said to them), "guzø-kewagam" (she looked after them,
  1Ti 5:10), "nøtaqá-pavát" (I will follow you, Luk 9:57), "qazoni-matanam" (you did to us,
  Luk 2:48), "ndozonø-akhayam" (you call me, Luk 6:46), "nøkhàna᷄z" (don't touch me, Jhn 20:17).
- Often the object is a free noun with no prefix: "Sævam zivis be-ìva᷄v" (1Co 7:10), "Nqova
  Mbomambaqape, Simeon ... genø-itú" (Luk 2:27), "Opa tugu zakhapuka sasa ndøngenøtam" built
  on "Yesu-té genøwáv ... sasa ndøngenøtam" (Mat 26:49).

### Tense, aspect, mood
- mø̀ndø- (a portmanteau subject prefix like ndø-, T1; "perfective, has already" is unconfirmed, m:møndø-:verb): "mø̀ndønaním" (Luk 18:3), "mø̀ndø-pakhaéz" (1Co 15:6),
  "mø̀ndø-fakhán", "mø̀ndæ-otév" (he understood, Mat 2:16 gloss); é-møndø- (still earlier).
- ndø- (3rd person portmanteau, T1; tense and statement/question by tone, narrative_tense.2): "ndøfakhán", "ndøwav", "sasa ndøqavan" (she returned, Luk 1:56),
  "ndø-okɨ́" (he married, Mrk 12:20), "ndøngazém" (they began, Luk 15:24).
- Emphatic past te-/tu-/ti- + ne/ni: "tènenaním" (he died, Act 7:60), "tùni-ová" (she gave
  birth, Luk 2:6), "tuni-ngæmam-év" (she became a widow, Luk 2:37), "tini-einím".
- gè- (completed, any subject; not yet checked with the team) and sège- (counterexpectational, T11, m:sege-:verb.team): "gè-iváv" (he left, Luk 2:4), "Ndugu gèvín" (she wept,
  Luk 7:38), "gè-eivám" (they wept, Act 20:37), "sègeqóm" (stayed behind, Luk 2:43),
  "gèmø-tøkenøvém" (clung to them, Act 3:11).
- Future: high tone on the stem: "ndǿgoát" (will be, Luk 1:32), "ndø̀fakhán", "nǿnaním"
  (I will die, Luk 15:17), "genǿwáv" (he will go, Luk 9:51), "zógoát" (you pl will be,
  1Pe 4:10), "sǽqotát" (I will stay, 1Co 16:6), "qatǿqaván" (I will go back, Luk 15:18),
  "qazinǿ-qaván" (let us go back, Jhn 11:7).
- Irrealis/subordinate (if, that, when): tæ- (I): "tæ-ein", "tændap" (1Co 9:17), "tægoat"
  (Luk 15:19); toqo-/toqote- (you): "toqote-vewat" (Act 10:15), "toqo-okɨ"; te- (he):
  "tenanim" (Mrk 12:19); tu- (she): "tugoat" (1Co 7:40); ti- (they): "tigoat" (Act 9:21),
  "ti-ova"; tozo- (you pl): "tozo-ometam" (Mat 2:8); tizi- (we).
- "Even if": "Oskiá ndøgo, qo namba tøtenanim" (Mat 26:35).
- Negation: suffix -áv / -eáv / -teáv / -máv: "etopøteáv" (did not give, Act 7:5), "ometeáv"
  (did not find, Luk 2:45), "pouweáv" (did not urge, Act 21:14), "zøtezateáv" (did not know);
  negative future -ák: "gemǿ-navøemák" (Luk 1:33), "nóqøuvaqák" (I will never leave you,
  Heb 13:5); "mbaín" = there is none / without: "nakheis mbaín" (Luk 1:7).

### Commands, wishes, prohibitions
- 2sg: "mòqoqáv" (go!, Act 7:34), "qanøqáv" (go to ..., Jhn 20:17), "Qakeza khoev-té qanøqáv"
  (Mrk 8:26), "vø̀khofoé" (take off!, Act 7:33).
- 2pl: "Vø̀ndozáv" (come!, Luk 15:6), "Zion-te nqawa tozovesez" (go back to John!, Luk 7:22),
  "sozó-okhoát" (go!, Mrk 16:15), "Zøtøndé-yogēm" (listen!, Act 7:2), "Gèqeiví" (look!).
- 1pl: "Bìziniáv" (let us go, Luk 2:15), "Sà bizimuniáv" (Jhn 11:16).
- Obligative be- (T2, m:be-:verb.team; wishes and 'let him' are its uses): "bezø-matanám" (may he do for them, glossary Balam), "Matev sà befakhán"
  (Luk 1:38), "bè-iváv" (let him leave), "bezøgó" (may it be for them), "yà beto-eqawát"
  (Luk 1:68), "Evezøza-qá poev mba begó" (Act 21:14).
- Prohibition: stem with grave on the penult and ᷄ on the last vowel, object prefix kept:
  "Qo møe ndø̀go᷄" (don't be afraid, Luk 1:13), "Zo møe ndøgòne᷄m" (Luk 2:10), "Qo ndø̀-ei᷄v"
  (don't weep, Luk 7:13), "zo ezoqa ‘Tat’ mbo-akhàya᷄m" (do not call anyone Father,
  Mat 23:9), "nøkhàna᷄z" (Jhn 20:17), "Sævam zivis be-ìva᷄v" (1Co 7:10).

## In Ruth and Jonah (human, 2015)

What the OT confirms, corrects and adds:

- **Confirmed**: subject prefixes (ge-/gu-/gi-, qa- for 'it': "qambo-ewez" it filled her, RUT
  2:14), object prefixes, -nø-/-ndo- direction, the vø̄- chain, the future by tone (tøtǿnáv,
  tǽyagát, tæmǿnaním 'I will go, live, die', RUT 1:16-17; ndæ̀wí 'I will buy', 4:4), negation
  -áv/-eáv ('pusa-qase agé taeveáv' she did not rest, 2:7; 'Niniva taon ngi꞉wateáv', JON 3:10),
  the negative future -ák ('pusa naqanøká gétayák', RUT 3:18; 'gemǿ-navøemák', 4:10), and the
  conditional with tøgoat ('poeveav tøgoat', 3:13, 4:4).
- **Prohibitive** (prohibitive.3): the NT-only rule was right. Nine OT stems: nøpòuwe᷄t (do not
  urge me, RUT 1:16), ndø̀qa᷄v (do not go, 2:8), ndø̀go᷄ (do not be, 3:11), ndøqàva᷄q (do not go
  back, 3:17), mbøe-khafène᷄m (pl, do not drive her away, 2:15), mbo-eìni᷄m (pl, do not say to her,
  2:16), nø-akhàya᷄m (pl, do not call me, 1:20), ndølòge᷄ (do not eat, JON 3:7), ni-etòa᷄m (do not
  give us, JON 1:14). The stem's final consonant comes after the mark.
- **Negative obligative** (T2: 'must not'): be- + the prohibitive tone = 'let him not / let no one':
  "Geté Boaz beqàme᷄kh." (but let Boaz not see you, RUT 3:3), "Ezoqa nøme betezø̀te᷄z av nqægo,
  ..." (let no one know that ..., 3:14), "lou neka ibøkha belòge᷄" (let them not eat or drink,
  JON 3:7). Our agents used a negative future here.
- **Obligative** be- (T2): 'let me die' "É-batenaním." (JON 4:3), "Bànønaním." (4:8); 'let him marry
  you' "beqakɨ́" (RUT 3:13); 'let him finish eating' "bembøe-navøém" (3:3); 'may the LORD do good
  to you' "ndø̀matanám" (1:9).
- **Hortative** 1pl (new forms; zi = 1pl inclusive, T4): zí- / sizi- / qazinǿ-: "Iziz zípeawám ... qazinǿ-nqeitøvém ...
  zíqeivím" (let us write, put, see; JON 1:7), "Sizió-kewagét" (let us wait, RUT 3:18), "lou
  logemav matev zígoném" (let us fast, JON 3:5). NT: bizi-.
- **Imperative** (imperative.3): "qonǿqáv" (go there, JON 1:2, RUT 3:3), "Mòqosuzám" (get up,
  JON 1:6), "Qóvøndáz" (hold it, RUT 3:15), "sasa qóbūv" (lie down, 3:4), "Lou qólóg" (eat,
  2:14), "Vø̀khæ-khakuám" (wash, 3:3); pl "Qazonǿ-qavøním" (go back, 1:8), "Qànøqonáv nqanek"
  (sit here, 4:2), "Zóqambúz" (turn, JON 3:8).
- **-ɨn** (suffix_in, new): marks what is not (yet) real: a request with Ambá ("No ambá khae-té
  qatønavɨ́n" let me go to the field, RUT 2:2; "No ambá qonøkeɨ́n" please marry me, 3:9), an
  attempt or near-event with amba ("amba Tasis manqei-te tækhokhonɨn" I wanted to flee to
  Tarshish, JON 4:2; "palai khagua ambá vø̄tøkuiamin" the ship nearly broke apart, 1:4), a
  hypothesis (RUT 4:15), and customs: "Ezoqa ... ge-ivavɨn, ndego tamak kopo ndøkhofaɨ́n, ndego
  ezoqa nøme vømbō-etoamɨn." (a man would give ..., would take off his sandal, would give it,
  4:7). This answers our gap:custom_explanation.
- **Ability** (new): "no kopømba mbaín manqei tæwi" (I cannot buy the land, RUT 4:6), "Geté
  kopømba mbaín." (but they could not, JON 1:13); "kopømbá" (that is fine, you may; RUT 4:4).
- **Lie / sleep**: lie (down) is buv, not sømbi ("sasa qóbūv", "segebúv", "gubuvam", RUT 3);
  sleep = "venanqei buv". The first version of this page and our drafts used sømbi.

Correction to the object-marking section above: "Opa tugu zakhapuka sasa ndøngenøtam" was our
built sentence; the human has "Opa ... zakhapuka-pakha sège-møvøiú, ouo manqat vømbō-ein" (RUT
1:14), and ngenøtam is used for holding a child (4:16).

## Wishes for things: be- and bete- (Genesis 1, agent draft gen001)

Genesis 1 is a chain of third-person commands to things ("let the earth bring forth", "let
the lights shine"). The corpus has two obligative (T2) shapes for them: be- + stem ("begó" let it be,
"befakhan" let it appear, "beqatøzo" let it separate) and bete-/betø- + stem ("Wit ... sa
bete-umianét" let both grow, Mat 13:30; "betekháev" let it shine, Mat 5:16). The draft uses
bete- where a model has it (bete-umianét, betekháev) and builds "bete-løvuvat" (let them fly)
on that pattern; no obligative of løvuv- is attested. The hortative 'let us make' is zí- +
khakhein, "zíkhakheinám", by the pattern of "Iziz zípeawám" (let us write, Jon 1:7).

- **Present with nq-**, mostly first person: "nqazø-manqate" (I tell you pl, 141×), as in

## The team's answers (26.10.2026)

- **ndø-** (T1) is one of over 700 portmanteau subject prefixes, normally 3rd person (sg or pl); the **tone
  pattern** tells past/present from future and statement from question, so "neutral/narrative ndø-" above names a use,
  not its meaning (claim:ndø-.team). A grave on the second-last syllable and U+1DC4 on the last make a 2nd-person "do
  not" command (claim:prohibitive.team, as prohibitive.3).
- **be-** (T2) is **obligative**, not optative: grave on its first syllable + acute on the last = affirmative; grave on
  the second-last + U+1DC4 on the last = "must not"; a macron on its last syllable before the stem = sequential
  (claim:be-.team, obligative.team, negative_obligative.team, sequential_obligative.team). The wishes above are uses of it.
- **sege-** (T11) marks a **counterexpectational** verb (claim:sege-.team), not "completed" as the list above says.
- **gu-** (T10) is a feminine subject, and is commonly used also when a woman is the object or indirect object
  (claim:gu-.team, as verb_subject_prefix.2).
- **ndo** (T3) is an allative marker, "coming from" in the team's words (claim:-ndo-.team).
- **vø̄- / vø̄e-** (T12) are both sequential forms of "say": vø̄-ein "and he/she said", vø̄e-ein "and I said"; after
  até, vø̄e-ein is "he/she thought"; like av, ate takes the referential/ablative verb form (ate_ablative.team).
- The **hyphen** (T1) has no grammatical meaning ([writing](../orthography.md)).
