# Looking ahead: Genesis 31–50

The key words of the Jacob and Joseph chapters, with what Genesis 1–30 or the NT already shows.
Where neither has a model the entry is a `gap` with a proposal (`status: open`): build the word
as proposed and list it in the chapter notes for the team. Names are on [names](names.md), the
narrative formulas on [OT narrative](ot-narrative.md).

## Genesis 48: Jacob blesses Ephraim and Manasseh (agent gen048)

- The blessing has two wordings, both kept apart: a man's hand-laying blessing *zenda gembøeve* /
  *zenda gezømbove* (HEB 11:21 tells this very scene: "Zekop Zosef-gi nakhei angana menas zenda
  gezømbove"), and God's blessing with *khakheinøv*: "no gekhakheinømbem" (he blessed me, Laban's words in
  GEN 30:27), the wish *bèkhakheinøvém* (may he bless them) and *bèkhakheinøqavém* (you).
- Jacob's blindness follows Isaac's in GEN 27:1 (*bøi waev vømbø̄i-navøem*); "my son" is *Áti!* as there.
- "The God before whom my fathers walked" follows GEN 24:40 and 17:1: walking before God is keeping his
  word (*manqat o-mba … ndap*).
- "Redeemed me from all harm" is told with Paul's "the Lord rescued me from all of them" (2TI 3:11,
  *mø̀ndø-khandí꞉n*); the kinsman's *nqovotáz* (term:H1350) stays open.
- Gaps: crossing the hands (48:14), 'bring you back' with God as subject (48:21).

## Genesis 49: Jacob's blessings and death (agent gen049)

- The blessings are poetry; the team's own blessings and curses in GEN 9:25–27 and 27:27–29, 39–40 give the
  wordings: "cursed be" *bèsoqoév*, "let them praise" *yàbeto-eqawát*, "bow down to you" *qoqa megemege-te
  bēfoyamat*, "you will not be" *qógoák* (for Reuben's "you will no longer excel").
- The animal likenesses stay likenesses with *andé X me* where the corpus has the animal (lion *layon*, donkey
  *donki*, snake *wazeam*, wolf *gaqo khobos*, JHN 10:12); where it has none (doe) or no matching word
  (hamstring, dark eyes) the line is told by its meaning and the store has a gap.
- The noun "blessing(s)" (25–26) is told as *gigiap eqeieqei* (good things God gives); the verb keeps the two
  wordings of Genesis 48.
- Jacob's death follows the team's Abraham (GEN 25:8–10) word for word: *ekeza ezoqam zøpaév, ndigu
  bugukhokhof go꞉pakhaez* (gathered to his people), the cave *Makpela nandi nqa*, the field bought from the
  Hittites as *manqei pakha upøgim nonqo* (23:20).
- A name in the vocative takes the accent on its first syllable (*Lúben!*, *Zúda!*), as *Evézøza* and *Zíona*.
- **Question for the Kuni team:** Shiloh (49:10) as a name *Sailo*, or "the one to whom it belongs"?


## Genesis 50: Jacob's burial, Joseph's forgiveness and death (agent gen050)

- The burial follows the team's Genesis: mourning *mutøkhop neka eiv matev ndøgó* (GEN 23:2), loud wailing *eiv neka
  osombo kandambá* (MRK 5:38), the cave at Machpelah (GEN 25:9), "they carried him" *ginø-tonqogimám* (ACT 7:16).
- No word for embalming: told as wrapping the body with spices (JHN 19:39–40); physicians *mulømula etoam ezoqam*
  (COL 4:14); coffin as a wooden bier *tae kumøkuma* (LUK 7:14, built).
- Forgiveness as in the Lord's Prayer and on the cross: *sà betaqambo-navøemát* (LUK 11:4), *qo sà betaqambo-navøém*
  (LUK 23:34); "do not be afraid" *møe ndøgòne᷄m* (binding prohibitive); "Am I in the place of God?" in the team's own
  words of GEN 30:2 *Gê, no Mbumbukiàm?*
- Joseph's last words follow HEB 11:22, which retells 50:25: *manqei tozo-ivøvem, noqa bazag vø̀-upatét*.
- Names: *Atad*, *Eibol-Mizlaim* (Abel-mizraim; the pun not bracketed), *Makil* (Machir). Question for the team:
  should 'across the Jordan' be 'on the other side' rather than 'near' (*avønin-te*)?

## The Joseph story in the NT

Stephen tells Joseph's story in ACT 7:9–16, so its words are attested in Kuni: Joseph sold into
Egypt, "Felo-qa megemege-te" (before Pharaoh, 7:10), the famine "vikia sekeqape ndømát" (7:11),
"lou zua ndøgō" (there was food, i.e. grain, in Egypt, 7:12), Joseph making himself known to
"ekeza zapikia-za" (his brothers, 7:13), seventy-five people (seventi-fáev, 7:14), the tomb at
Shechem bought with silver (7:16). Read those verses before Gen 37, 41–45 and 50.

## Terms

- **dream**: "venanqei tam-te" (in a dream, GEN 20:3; MAT 1:20, 2:13 for Joseph the
  carpenter's dreams); "he dreamed" = "venanqei ndøtám" (28:12), followed by "Yaq nqáe!" and
  what he saw. Plural by context.
- **interpret**: a gap. The word for a name's meaning, "taqa manqa mokho", and the disciples'
  "explain to us" (MAT 15:15, "mokho toqoti-nømand") are the closest models.
- **famine**: "vikia seke(qape) ndømát" (12:10; ACT 7:11; RUT 1:1), and "lou lou-av matev"
  (the food-lack matter, 26:1). Gen 41–47 have about 25 famine verses: vary as the team does.
- **grain**: *wit* (27:28 "wit neka waen" grain and new wine); the grain bought in Egypt is
  food, *lou*, in ACT 7:12.
- **silver**: *silva* (13:2, 23:16), money *moni* (17:12), price *fia*. The 20 pieces of silver
  (37:28) and the silver cup (44:2) by these.
- **brothers**: one brother *eveqase*, the brothers *zapikia-za* (term:H251.g,
  term:H251brothers.g). Benjamin, "his mother's son" (43:29): "ekeza evo-ge yo" by the
  possessive rule.
- **prison**: *ndimbula* (NT only, 111 verses), the jailer "ndimbula keoge ezoqam" (ACT 16:23).
- **steward**: "sasae kawa ezoqam" (Abraham's chief servant, 24:2).
- **mourn, weep**: "mutøkhop neka eiv matev" (23:2); weep *vin-* (gèvín, 27:38).
- **bury, tomb, cave**: "manqei pakha … upa" (23:4, 23:19) or *otøv-* (25:9); the cave "nandi
  nqa", "Makpela nandi nqa" (25:9) for 49:29–32 and 50:13.
- **coat** (Joseph's robe): built in Genesis 37 as "ndabua sekeqape, khagi vinivinimba-ús" (a long
  garment with many colours; term:coat_ornate.g, a question for the team); clothes are *ndabua* (27:15),
  torn clothes "ndabua vø̄keqa" (MRK 14:63).
- **household gods** (31:19–35): still a gap. Our Genesis 31 draft used *tae khøuwiap* (carved images, ACT 7:41) for the narrator and *mbumbuk* (pagan gods) in Laban's "my gods" (gap:household_gods.g31).
- **animals and camp**: camel *kemol*, donkey *donki*, cattle *kao*, goats *naningus*, sheep
  *sip*, tent *palai (khoev)*.
- **Pharaoh, Egypt**: *Felo* (12:15; ACT 7:10), "Izip manqei" (12:10), an Egyptian "Izip
  manqei-ak" (16:1); Pharaoh is "Izip kawa ezoqam" (the king of Egypt, 12:16).

## Genesis 31 (drafted 15.10.2026, agent:gen031)

What the chapter needed and where the models are:

- **Laban and Jacob quarrel in the words of Gen 29–30.** "What have you done to me?" is the team's
  "Gekha zapâ, matev av nqægo qoqonø-matanam?" (12:18); "you deceived me" is *qoqonømbe-khavoz*
  (29:25); Jacob's anger is "mbøni mbonqosøgím" (30:2); "you know how I served you" is close to
  30:26. Reusing these keeps the voices the same.
- **Covenant scene.** Gen 21:22–33 and 26:26–31 (Abimelech) are the models: *poev … unimanqatin-*
  for the covenant, *manqa mbusa … ve* for the oath, "lou loge-qa zø-akhá" (29:22) for the meal.
  "between you and me" is always "yaq qo-a, yaq no" (13:8, 23:15). "May God judge between us"
  follows Sarai's wish in 16:5 (*segé-geveá* … "qô o nô").
- **The heap and the pillar.** No word for a heap of stones was found: the draft says the men
  gathered stones "into one place" (GEN 1:9, *kopo-te*) and then calls them "these stones". The
  pillar is 28:18 verbatim ("Andé uk me me꞉wuáb"). A thing as **witness** has no model; the draft
  used *khakhatap* (sign, the covenant sign of 9:12), and *bøi vizu ezoqam* where God is the witness.
  Both are questions for the team.
- **Names given at the heap** get the team's brackets: Galed "(Iblu manqat-te …)", after the team's
  own note on Reuben (29:32), and Zega-Saaduta "(Alam manqat-te.)" for the Aramaic name.
- **Counterfactuals** ("if God had not been with me, you would have sent me away empty") use the
  -ɨn pattern of JHN 15:24: "amba … tegonɨn, … ambá sège…ɨ́n".
- **Colours of the flock.** Gen 30 has *løvugiap* and *bokhotap-us* for three Hebrew words; the
  draft added "khagi papaqa-qasis" (with white on it, 30:35) for the third.
- Built and open: *gèmbo-ten* (ten times), the saddlebag, "the way of women", tambourine (left out),
  "bore the loss", "squandered". Each has a gap entry with what was searched.

## Genesis 32 (drafted 15.10.2026, agent:gen032)

- **Messengers are people, not angels.** The NT's *u-anim* (LUK 9:52 "u-anim ndøkhofotáz, teqa
  megemege-te", LUK 7:10 the messengers returned) fits Jacob's messengers word for word; *enzol
  ezoqam* stays for "the angels of God" (32:2).
- **Messages within messages.** Jacob's "say to my lord Esau: 'Thus says your servant Jacob: …'"
  follows the disciples' instructions in MAT 21:3 and MAT 26:18: "āv qazømbøé-einím nqǽgo, ‘… āv
  gene-eín: …’", the inner message after a colon, without a third level of quotes. "If he asks you …,
  say …" is LUK 19:31. The 2sg "āv qombøé-eín nqǽgo" (32:19) is built.
- **The prayer (32:10–13)** reuses the team's promise wording: "I am small" is Ruth's "No ezoqam
  khapúmbá" (RUT 2:13), the offspring that cannot be counted is 16:10 and 13:16 ("kopoav-qape
  ndø̀taqa-etoám … gevebumav ndǿgoát"), the sand of the sea ROM 9:27. "Help me" is MAT 15:25
  "Vø̀nøtøké". "Go back to your land" is "qonǿveséz" (3:19); gen031's "mòqoveséz" is not in the corpus.
- **Camps.** No noun for a camp exists in the corpus (HEB 13:11–13 says "outside the town"). The draft
  calls the groups *bawan* and explains Mahanaim as "(Taqa manqa mokho: Bawan menás.)"; dividing them
  is *qatøz-* (21:28) as "Møvøyap foá" (four parts, JHN 19:23). Question for the team.
- **The gift** (מִנְחָה) is Cain and Abel's *etoam matev* (4:3–5), the same Hebrew word. "Appease him"
  is Rebekah's "until his anger subsides" (27:44 "mbøni nqosøgeap bembø̄e-navøem"); "perhaps he
  will accept me" is Ruth's "tanakh ndø̀nøgó" (RUT 2:2).
- **Animals by sex**: the team writes *angana* / *sakheis* after the animal (30:35, 12:16); 200 = "tu
  andled" (11:23).
- **The wrestling.** "Let me go" is Jacob's own "É-moqonøták" to Laban (30:25); "left alone" is JHN
  8:9; the hip is Abraham's *bov zapazapa* (24:2); limping is "bov gèmbo-wauwauám" (ACT 14:8). The
  fight is *nøfeap* (GAL 5:17, HEB 11:33), "you have prevailed" is built on Rachel's "mø̀e-kopømba-én"
  (30:8). The renaming follows 17:5 ("Qoqa iz, Eiblam sekemba gégoák … Geté Eibleem qágoát"), with
  Israel's meaning in brackets. "Why do you ask my name?" is MAT 19:17.
- **Gaps:** ford (left out), dislocated (rendered as crippled), nursing camels (with their young),
  tendon = *kinik* (ligament, COL 2:19, two verses only).

## Genesis 33 (drafted 15.10.2026, agent:gen033)

- **The meeting is told with the team's greeting words.** Esau runs, embraces and kisses as Laban
  does in 29:13 ("nakhamas-té … sège-møvøiú, vø̄ngenøtam"); "fell on his neck" is not rendered
  separately (the embrace covers it). Jacob bows as Abraham in 18:2 ("gèkhafutáv … Bugug até
  manqei-té"); the women and children bow with the plural of REV 7:11 ("ekeza kha, manqei me꞉laém").
  "Seven times" puts the numeral before the verb ("seven gèkhafutáv", as LUK 17:4 counts acts):
  open, like gen031's *gèmbo-ten*.
- **Order of the line**: first *bugukhokhof*, then "Taqa zita-te", last "Yaq vaev-te" (1CO 15:8,
  LUK 20:32). The same "vaev-te" gives "and then Joseph and Rachel" (33:7).
- **The polite exchange** reuses Genesis' own bargaining scenes: "No, please" is Lot's "Āv taoká"
  (19:3), "accept it" is Abraham's "sègekhatiú" (23:13), "keep what is yours" is the king of Sodom's
  "bètaqagó" (14:21), "Jacob pressed him" is Lot's "gèzø-pouweám, bazaføgakh-ús" (19:3, here
  gèmbo- for one person). Favour is *tanakh* throughout ("qo tanakh toqonøgo", gen032:6), and "God
  has been gracious to me" is the NT's "have mercy on me" turned into a past ("Mbumbukiam no tanakh
  genøgo", after PHP 2:27 "tanakh mbogó").
- **"My lord knows …"** becomes the vocative plus "you know" (JHN 21:17 "mòqote-qatéq"), as Ruth
  turns "my lord" into *Khanánqa*. "Let us go" is Cain's "qazinǿ-niáv" (4:8); "I will go ahead of
  you" is MAT 26:32 with *qoqa*; "until I come" is 1TI 4:13; "slowly" is ACT 27:7 *aiyav-té*.
- **Animals**: ewes and cows "nakhei tete ndizøloge" (nursing, LUK 21:23). "Driven hard", "in my
  care" and "at the pace of" have no model: the draft says "if I lead them fast", "with me" and "as
  they walk" (gap entries).
- **Succoth and the altar**: shelters are the NT's *khoev-qasis* (LUK 9:33, the Feast of Tabernacles
  JHN 7:2), so Succoth's meaning in brackets is "(Taqa manqa mokho: Khoev-qasis.)". Jacob camps as
  Isaac does (26:25 "palai khoev vø̄puan") and buys the field as Abraham does (23:16–20 "X-te gewi",
  the silver given with *vøzø̄-etoam*); Hamor is the NT's *Hamo* (ACT 7:16). El-Elohe-Israel is
  written *El-Eloe-Izlael* with its meaning in brackets.

## Genesis 34 (drafted 15.10.2026, agent:gen034)

- **The rape of Dinah is told plainly but with the team's own words.** "He took her" is Pharaoh's
  *guindaf* (12:17, gu- because a man acts on a woman); "lay with her" is the wedding-night phrase
  "venanqei nonqo vø̄tøkuanem" (29:23); "by force" is added as "she did not want" (the servant's
  *poeveav*, 24:5) plus *bazaføgakh-te* (by strength). "Defiled" has no model for a person: the
  draft says "he did an immoral act with her" with REV 18:3 ("tu namba mambe matev"); the brothers'
  word "prostitute" is *mambe sævam* (1CO 6:16 "mambe okho sævam"), so the chapter's two words stay
  in one family. **Question for the Kuni team:** how plainly may a Kuni reader be told this?
- **Anger and the outrage**: "very angry" is the NT's "qaqa kandambá gigonem" (ACT 19:28); grief is
  "mbøni vaev matev" with zø- (them), as gen032's distress; "a thing that should not be done" is
  Abimelech's "Ezoqa matønumáv, matev av nqægo" (20:9).
- **Negotiation at the gate** follows Genesis 23 (the gate = "taon mboqog tambav", those who came out
  of the gate = "ndigu taon mboqog-te ginduzav", 23:10, 18) and Genesis 24's marriage talk ("tegu
  sævam tugoat" as his wife, 30:9; "give her" as Laban, 29:19). Circumcision is the team's Genesis 17
  throughout: "angana ate ndi꞉gu, ngusu bèzøqogøzát" (17:10) for the condition, "ngusu vø̄qogaz"
  (17:27) for the deed; an uncircumcised man is "ndego ngusum kepøtøzateav gegoam" (1CO 7:18).
  "Only on this condition" is "Geté sa nqánek:" (ACT 15:28), the condition after a colon.
- **Trade** is the loan *bisnis* with *gonem* (JAS 4:13); "acquire property" became "buy land".
  "Dowry" has no word (gap: the draft says *fia*, price, as 23:15).
- **The massacre** uses Genesis 14's raid: goods carried off "vø̄-upøgimem" (14:11), people seized
  with *gean* (14:12), looting with *aqon* (steal, MAT 12:29); killing many is "pakhapakha
  vøzø̄-khanæmam" (MRK 12:5). "On the third day" is "Khøuwa misika-te ndøgo" (22:4).

## Genesis 43 (drafted 16.10.2026, agent:gen043)

The second journey, with Benjamin, and the meal in Joseph's house. Much of it is dialogue that the team's
Genesis 1–30 already has a model for:

- **Asking after someone.** "Is your father well?" is Jacob's question about Laban at the well (29:6,
  *Ndego kopømbâ tego?*), and the answer is the shepherds' *Ndego kopømbá ngeyage.* "He is still alive" is Paul's
  word over Eutychus (ACT 20:10, *ngekhandî꞉vát*) with *zua*; "do you have another brother?" follows LUK 24:41
  (*tøzǿgo*) and the angels' question to Lot (19:12). Genesis 44–46 ask the same questions.
- **Hospitality.** Water to wash the feet and food for the animals are word for word the team's 24:32 (Laban's
  welcome); the steward is *sasae kawa ezoqam* (24:2); "slaughter an animal" is the father's command in LUK 15:23
  (*vø̀ndolaém*); "he did as Joseph said" is MAT 1:24; the gifts are presented and the brothers bow as the magi do
  (MAT 2:11). "Peace to you" became *Ság.* (fine, 30:15), a question for the team.
- **Counting and comparing.** "N times" is *khøuwa* + the numeral (2CO 11:24 *Khøuwa faev*): this settles the
  open "ten times" / "seven times" gaps of Genesis 31 and 33. Benjamin's portion uses the comparative
  *mø̀ndøløvú*. The lexicon gives *khobos* for "double", but *khobos* means fierce (a roaring lion, 1PE 5:8), so
  "double the silver" is built as *moni bawan menás* (two lots of money).
- **Judah's pledge and the counterfactual.** No word for being surety was found; Paul's offer to pay Onesimus's debt
  (PHM 1:19) is the nearest model, and the draft says "I will look after him; you may ask me about him"
  (gap:surety.g43). "If we had not delayed, we would have come back twice" follows JHN 15:22 (*okhoeav tøgonɨn …
  ambá …-ɨ́n*) with *føløtaz* (delay, HEB 10:37).
- **The meal.** Separately = *yakhapús* (2:18); "detestable to the Egyptians" = forbidden, *khafeáp* (MAT 12:4);
  the inner room is *khoev mokho* (MAT 6:6); the brothers' astonishment is *nqova zøndáf* (MRK 9:15). "They drank
  freely (became drunk) with him" is told as eating, drinking and great joy, since the NT's drinking words describe
  drunkenness as a vice (EPH 5:18).
- **Built and open:** surety, mistake, double, pistachios and almonds, best products, "intend to overpower",
  "received", full weight, composure, portions, drinking freely (gaps above); built verbs *ndæ̀kewág*,
  *qazindu-qavazɨ́n*, *ginigeanemɨ́n*, *ndønøzøgím*, *betaqagó*, *gilógát*.

## Genesis 44 (drafted 16.10.2026, agent:gen044)

- **The cup and divination.** The cup is *kap* (silver cup = "silva kap"). Divination is the NT's
  sorcery: Simon "manqa-sisi matev ndømatønumám" (ACT 8:9), the same noun as the magicians of Gen 41
  (*manqa-sisi ezoqam*). Joseph's "a man like me can divine" is built from it with *kopømbaqapé*.

  (ROM 12:17 *soqain … qavø̀ina᷄m*); "what you have done is wicked" follows REV 3:2 ("Matev ndøgo, zo
  ndozo-matanam, …"); "you shall be free of blame" is 1CO 1:8 ("manqa-zapazapa mbaín, sozógoát").

  Abraham's "Kopømba mbaín, …" (GEN 18:25). "How can we justify ourselves?" is Paul's "I cannot say:
  I am right" (1CO 4:4).

  angry" (GEN 18:30, 32). "Your servant(s)" is kept where the Hebrew stresses it and otherwise turned
  into *no* / *ni*, as the store's humble_self says; "my lord" in the third person becomes *qo* (you),
  as Ruth and the team do (RUT 2:13, GEN 18:3). The 1pl → 2sg "we answered you" (*qeitaqa-qavøinam*)
  is built from *qei-* (2CO 7:14) and *taqa-* (you sg): a question for the team.
- **Family words from Genesis 21 and John 8**: "the child of his old age" (21:2), "my wife bore me
  two sons" (30:5–6, "gave me"), "he alone is left" (JHN 8:9), "I cannot bear to see" (Hagar, 21:16,
  "bøi bate-òme᷄t"), "bound up with his life" (1CO 10:16 "namba me꞉tøkunuín").

  us a little food" (43:2), the surety "No nakhasam ndæ̀kewág" (43:9), the gray hair to Sheol as the

## Genesis 45 (drafted 16.10.2026, agent:gen045)

- **"I am Joseph."** The name alone with a final accent, as Jacob's "No Isaó." (GEN 27:19): *No
  Zoséf.* "The one you sold into Egypt" follows Peter's "Here am I, the one you are looking for" (ACT
  10:21 *Ezoqam nó, …*); "I am the one speaking with you" (45:12) is JHN 9:37 in the first person.
  "Made himself known" is the NT's own wording for this scene (ACT 7:13 *tènezømás*).
- **Comfort and its images.** "Do not be distressed" is Jesus' "do not let your hearts be troubled"
  (JHN 14:1 *Zo mbøni bezø̀va᷄*), reused for "do not regret your goods" (45:20). Benjamin's neck goes
  (the team's 29:13 *sège-møvøiú*, embraced him); "made me a father to Pharaoh" is kept as a likeness
  (*andé Felo-ge eve me nogó*), like "you are equal to Pharaoh" in 44:18: a question for the team
  whether to explain it as "adviser". "To preserve life" and "a great deliverance" both use
  *khandi꞉zat-qa* (to save, 1TI 2:6).
- **Narrative from Genesis 1–30**: Isaac sends Abimelech's men away (26:31 *gekhofotaz, giqavaz*);
  Esau wept aloud (27:38, *gèvín. Eiv gèmbøe-eqavát.*); "before I die" is Isaac's *Okhokho-té
  qabatø-naním.* (27:4); "you will live" (in Goshen) is the team's *qóyagét* (12:12, 20:7).
- **Jacob's reaction**: "his heart went numb" = *nqova mbondáf* (startled, amazed: LUK 1:12); "his
  spirit revived" is built from Paul's "they refreshed my spirit" (1CO 16:18 *kha vøndæv*), low
  certainty; *khanakhanakh ndøndáp* (became glad) is the alternative.
- **New words**: wagons have no word; the chariot term *manqei khagua wil-us* is used for them (ACT
  8:28). Goshen = *Gosen manqei* (by the letter rules). Grandchildren as a pair with children = 1TI 5:4
  *nakheis neka zi*. "Do not quarrel on the way" uses the attested negative optative *beponqòmba᷄p*
  (TIT 3:2).
- **Style**: the style check counts *manqei* high (18 for about 6), because Genesis writes "Izip
  manqei", "Keinan manqei" and the wagon term; the NT verses it compares with name places without
  *manqei*. Left as the team's Genesis does it.
- Gaps: destitution (*gigiap-av ezoqam*, the poor), plowing and harvest, remnant. Least sure: v7, v8,
  v11, v19, v27.
